Language work led by the people who speak the language.

The Forum for Language Initiatives is a non-profit in Islamabad, founded in 2002. We train and equip the indigenous communities of northern Pakistan to document, develop and sustain their own languages and cultures.

We are at the forefront of indigenous language preservation in our region.

We put mother-tongue speakers in the driving seat of language development and cultural documentation, by giving them the training they need and the equipment to do the work, so that indigenous languages are served sustainably.

FLI works with the minority and endangered language communities of Chitral, Gilgit-Baltistan, Swat, Kohistan, Dir, Hazara, Azad Kashmir and South Waziristan. Community researchers trained by FLI write the orthographies, the books and the keyboard layouts themselves; FLI provides the method, the training and the printing.

Our work covers writers' and orthography workshops, language documentation, mother-tongue education, publishing, digital tools, and a network of community-based organisations and partners at home and abroad.

Why we exist.

Our vision and mission were set by FLI's Board and guide every programme we run.

Our vision

To see all ethnolinguistic communities in Pakistan flourishing culturally and economically.

Our mission

To empower ethno-linguistic communities of Pakistan to strengthen their linguistic and cultural heritages.

Our core values

Centrality of languages and culture to identityEmpowerment, partnership and collaborationRespect, inclusiveness and pluralityEmpathy and compassionIntegrity, transparency and accountabilityKnowledge sharing

Our goal: empowered local communities with diverse and vibrant cultural and linguistic heritages.

Five objectives turn that goal into work we can measure.

  1. Mother-tongue languages are used across socio-cultural and social media domains within targeted minority ethnolinguistic communities.
  2. Mother-tongue languages are included in education by the provincial governments.
  3. Children in targeted communities are accessing non-curriculum reading materials in their mother tongue, including in digital formats and on social media.
  4. Businesses are supported in promoting local cultural tourism, cultural music and events, and local arts and crafts in targeted minority ethnolinguistic communities.
  5. Formalised and effective support from a broad range of organisations and institutions for mother-tongue language development at local and national levels.
