Endangered languages and oral traditions.

FEL XXVIII, the 28th conference of the Foundation for Endangered Languages, held in Islamabad by FEL, FLI and Allama Iqbal Open University.

Conference programme
25–28 Sep 2024Dates
AIOU, IslamabadVenue
HybridIn person and online
ConcludedStatus

Oral traditions and expressions are the collective memory of a community, and play a crucial role in keeping languages and cultures alive.

They come in a huge variety of spoken forms: folk songs, stories, riddles, tales, legends, myths, poems, epics, chants, charms and romances, often performed by lay members of the community who pass on their skills from generation to generation. When languages are threatened and become extinct, these traditions are permanently lost.

Selected papers are to be published in the FEL–Brill Endangered Languages Series.

FEL XXVIII examined the relationship between endangered languages and oral traditions: the role vivid oral traditions can play in revitalising languages, and the impact revitalisation can have on keeping oral traditions alive. With a universal scope, it set out to highlight the oral traditions of Pakistan's indigenous communities and the community initiatives preserving them.

Sub-themes

  • Endangered oral literatures: heritage preservation (music, poetry, mushaira, contests)
  • Oral cultures and traditional knowledge
  • Documentation and digitalisation of oral art and literature
  • Language policy, planning, and oral art
  • Oral art and mother-tongue education
  • Mother-tongue education policies: oral art and literature
  • Rediscovering oral traditions and expressions
  • Oral traditions as a vehicle for the transmission of culture and language
