Hindukush Cultural Conference in Chitral.

The 4th International Hindukush Cultural Conference (IHCC4), organised by Anjuman Taraqqi Khowar (ATK) and FLI, in continuation of the first conference held in Moesgaard, Denmark, in 1970.

The 4th International Hindukush Cultural Conference was an academic and scholarly activity in continuation of the first conference held in Moesgaard, Denmark, in 1970. The 2022 conference commemorated the golden jubilee of that first conference.

The 2nd International Hindukush Cultural Conference was hosted by the Anjuman in Chitral in August 1990, with Professor Israruddin (Chairman, Department of Geography, University of Peshawar) as convener and Dr Karl Jettmar (South Asian Institute, University of Heidelberg, Germany) as general president. The 3rd conference was hosted by the same organisation in Chitral in October 1995, with the same convener and Professor Schuyler Jones (Pitt Rivers Museum, England) as general president. The 4th conference was convened by Professor Israruddin to give high-profile scholars in the field of Hindukush studies an opportunity to meet and exchange ideas about their recent work.

The conference was planned for 2020, but could not take place because of the Covid-19 pandemic and was postponed again the following year. It was finally held on 14–16 September 2022.

Objective

To provide a platform to researchers, students and professionals from the development sector for discussing the social and economic issues and finding solutions through interaction, knowledge sharing and networking.

The conference

75 research papers, out of 85 originally submitted, were presented, and six keynote speeches were delivered. Papers were submitted through event-management software and blind peer-reviewed. Presenters who could not attend in person sent a recorded presentation and then answered questions at the end of it.

Sessions ran in four rooms over the three days. More than 200 people registered to participate. Two panel discussions took place in the main hall on the final day: Culture and Ethnic Tourism in the Hindukush Region, and Adaptation to Climate Change in Pakistan’s Mountain Region.

Themes

Most themes came from the previous editions. Mother-Tongue Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE) was added this time.

History

  • Pre-colonial history of Chitral, Mastuj and Yasin
  • Pre-colonial history of Gilgit, Hunza and Nager
  • Colonial history of Northern Pakistan
  • Historiography of the Chitral and Gilgit areas
  • Northern Pakistan in the days of independence and the accession of the northern polities (principalities and stateless communities)
  • History and distribution of Muslim confessions in the Hindukush/Karakorum
  • History of population movements and migrations in the Hindukush/Karakorum
  • Ancient trade routes
  • Historical buildings, forts, palaces, mosques, remarkable residences, tower-houses, notable burials, historical sites
  • Findings and prospects in the archaeology of the Hindukush/Karakorum
  • Post-state history of Chitral

Geography

  • Adaptation to climate change in Pakistan’s mountain regions
  • Challenges for sustainable development
  • Reducing the knowledge gap: recent studies in mountain research
  • Regional development of Chitral
  • Water towers of humankind: Hindukush and Karakoram revisited
  • Pastoralism in the Hindukush
  • Exchange relations between Chitral and Afghanistan
  • Cultural geography of the Hindukush

Cultural

  • Poetry in the local languages of the Hindukush/Karakorum: past and present
  • Oral traditions and genealogical knowledge about the past in local communities
  • Ethnic and linguistic minorities and their quest for identity
  • Music and dance in local traditions
  • Polo and other traditional sports
  • Tradition and innovation in self-organization of local communities
  • Rites of birth, marriage and burial: tradition versus innovation
  • The cultural impact of development interventions and new technologies
  • Traditional artisans and their crafts

Kalasha heritage

  • Permanence and change in traditional behaviours, practices and conceptions
  • Kalasha women between tradition and innovation
  • The process of conversion among the Kalasha in the past and in the present
  • The present practice of Kalasha traditional ritual
  • Development interventions by government and NGOs in Kalasha valleys and their impact on the culture
  • Cultural impact of tourism in the Kalasha valleys
  • The new Kalasha: the younger generations between tradition and modernity
  • Challenges and threats for Kalasha culture and identity

Languages

  • Language documentation, description and promotion
  • Challenges in implementing language-in-education policy
  • Causes of language endangerment in the Hindukush region
  • Language maintenance
  • Indigenous voices in popular culture (e.g. social media, fiction, poetry, film, hip hop)
  • Multilingualism in the Hindukush region

Mother Tongue Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE)

  • MTB-MLE theory and practices
  • Curriculum development
  • Mother-tongue education
  • Teaching kits
  • Practice from the field
  • Reading material
  • Challenges in mother-tongue education

Tourism

  • Ecotourism in the Hindukush and Karakorum region
  • Cultural festivals in the Hindukush and Karakorum region
  • Recreational and environment tourism
  • Historical and religious tourism
  • Culture and ethnic tourism
  • Adventure and wildlife tourism
  • Any other topic in line with tourism in the Hindukush and Karakorum region

Keynote speakers

Conference President

Dr. Elena Bashir

“Three Khowar-English dictionaries and some spelling issues”

Read more

Dr. Elena Bashir has lived much of her adult life since 1961 in Pakistan. Since 1973 she has been visiting Chitral regularly, first bringing students on field trips, then working on her doctoral dissertation on the Kalasha language, completed in 1988. She has also done field work on other indigenous languages of Pakistan, including Wakhi, Burushaski, Shina, Balti and Balochi. For the past several years she has been teaching Urdu at the University of Chicago (USA).

University of Florence, Italy

Dr. Alberto Cacopardo

“The Story of History: The Enigmatic Vicissitudes of Chitral Historiography”

Read more

Alberto Cacopardo is adjunct professor of anthropology at the University of Florence, Italy. He has carried out ethnographic research on various populations of Chitral and neighbouring areas over a span of several decades, starting in 1973. He has published various books and articles on the subject.

University of Florence, Italy

Dr. Augusto S. Cacopardo

“A Mythological Text from the Kalasha of Birir”

Read more

Augusto S. Cacopardo is Adjunct Professor of Cultural Anthropology at the University of Florence, Italy. He has conducted anthropological field research in Chitral since the 1970s under the aegis of ISMEO and ISIAO, first in Bumburet and Rumbur, then among the converted Kalasha communities of the Drosh area and the other linguistic minorities of Southern Chitral (Dameli, Gawar, Jashi, Palula). He participated in the 2nd and 3rd International Hindu Kush Cultural Conferences in 1990 and 1995.

Freie Universität Berlin

Prof. Dr. Hermann Kreutzmann

“Hindukush Matters – Chitral between Karakoram, Pamirs, and Wakhan”

Read more

Prof. Emeritus Dr. Hermann Kreutzmann held the Chair of Human Geography at Freie Universität Berlin, and from 2005 until his retirement in 2020 was Director of its Centre for Development Studies. His research focuses on Central and South Asia, with Pakistan as the prime focus: development studies, high mountain research, mobility and migration, political geography and minority issues.

Stockholm University, Sweden

Dr. Henrik Liljegren

“Numerals in the Hindukush: An endangered domain within endangered languages”

Read more

Dr. Henrik Liljegren is a researcher in linguistics at Stockholm University, where he received his PhD in 2008. He is one of the co-founders of FLI, where he served for several years while also conducting fieldwork in Pakistan, primarily in the Palula community of Chitral. He was leading a Swedish Research Council project documenting Gawarbati.

Convener

Prof. Israr-ud-Din

“Origin and Distribution of Central Asian Tribes and Clans in Chitral”

Read more

Former Professor and Chairman, Department of Geography, University of Peshawar, which he headed for 14 years. He was president of the Pakistan Geographical Association for 13 years and convener of the previous two editions of this conference, in 1990 and 1995 in Chitral. He has done extensive research on the tribes of the Hindukush, particularly those living in Chitral.

Organisers, supporters and thanks

Anjuman Taraqqi Khowar (ATK)

Founded by the literary people of Chitral in 1956, ATK is one of the oldest literary organisations of northern Pakistan. It hosted the 2nd and 3rd International Hindukush Cultural Conferences in 1990 and 1995 in Chitral, and co-organised IHCC4 with FLI.
