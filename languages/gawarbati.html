Gawarbati گوَرباتی

Gawarbati is an Indo-European language spoken by around 15,000 people in the mountainous and partly inaccessible border region between northern Pakistan and north-eastern Afghanistan.

Locally known as Aranduyiwar in Pakistan, it belongs to the Dardic branch of the Indo-Aryan family and is closely related to Shumashti, Dameli and Grangeali. Estimates of its speaker population range between 9,000 and 75,000; fewer than 7,000 live in Pakistan.

In 2016, FLI implemented a one-year preliminary documentation project on four languages of the region, including Gawarbati. The project produced initial publications, recorded ten folktales and trained community researchers in language documentation. Since then, Gawarbati researchers have continued to develop literature in their language, and FLI has published four more books, including the poetry collection of a late community poet.

Gawarbati is primarily spoken along the Kunar River in Afghanistan's Narai and Ghaziabad districts, and in Pakistan's Chitral district, particularly in Arandu village. Most speakers are farmers; timber is the second largest source of income. Today Gawarbati stands among FLI's most thoroughly documented target languages.
