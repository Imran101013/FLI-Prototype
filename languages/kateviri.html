Kamviri-Kateviri کتہ وری

Kateviri, also called Kati and Shekhani, belongs to the Nuristani cluster.

Locals in Chitral call it Bashgali-war, "the language of those who came from Bashgal". It is mainly spoken in Bashgal, Nuristan, on the Afghan side; its speakers migrated to Chitral in the late 1900s and settled in border villages such as Shekhandeh, next to the Bumburet valley, about 8 km from the Afghan border. Kamviri comes from the same group; non-speakers in Chitral call both communities Shekhani.

The main sources of income are herding, farming and government jobs. Literacy has been low, but the new generation now has access to government schools.

Since FLI began supporting Kateviri in 2022, local researchers led by Nasir Mansoor have produced six books in four years, all printed by FLI, including an alphabet book, proverbs, folktales, a phrasebook and a translated novel. In 2026 FLI published Aruswari ("tasty words"), the first poetry anthology in Kateviri, with Urdu, English and IPA translations.
