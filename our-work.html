From first workshop to first book.

FLI works with one language community at a time, in a sequence that hands the work to local researchers. Here is how it works, and what it has produced.

Six steps, led by the community.

The order matters: each step builds on the one before. Examples are from FLI's own reports.

Survey the language

A vitality survey or an Issues in Language Development workshop finds what the language needs first.

Bateri, 2021: vitality survey across identity, orality and literacy

Agree a writing system

Community researchers, guided by FLI, choose an orthography together. The community makes the decisions.

Madaklashti, June 2026: 17 participants, nine of them women

Train writers

Writers' workshops teach poets, teachers and students to write in their mother tongue.

Booni, September 2026: 50 teachers, half of them women

Publish

FLI prints the books community authors write: folktales, poetry, phrasebooks, primers.

Aruswari, 2026: the first poetry anthology in Kateviri

Go digital

Free keyboards and fonts let the language live on phones and social media.

2025: Android keyboards upgraded to 15 languages

Teach and advocate

Mother-tongue education, teacher training and networks that speak to government.

D.I. Khan, April 2026: Saraiki subject teaching for teachers
